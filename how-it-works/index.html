    How it works¶

    A score you can't interrogate isn't worth much. This page documents exactly what Focus Pulse measures, what it deliberately refuses to measure, and how the number on the gauge is calculated.

    What counts as browsing time¶

    Time is credited to a site only when all three of these hold:

    • The site's tab is the active tab in its window.
    • A Chrome window actually has focus.
    • The system is not idle or locked.

    The moment any of those stops being true, the clock stops and the elapsed time is banked against that site's hostname. When they become true again, a fresh session starts.

    Pages that are never tracked¶

    Internal browser pages are skipped entirely — chrome://, chrome-extension://, edge://, and about:. Time spent on the new tab page or in browser settings is not counted as browsing and never appears in your statistics.

    Hostnames, not URLs¶

    Only the hostname is stored. Reading three different articles on wikipedia.org produces one entry, wikipedia.org, with the combined time. The full address is used momentarily to extract that hostname and is not retained.

    Handling absence honestly¶

    The hardest part of a focus tracker is not counting time you weren't there for.

    Idle detection runs on a 5-minute threshold. Chrome's idle API only sees physical keyboard and mouse input, so a shorter threshold could punish normal behaviour — reading a long article without touching the mouse would register as absence. Five minutes is the trade-off: genuine idle time can take up to five minutes to be noticed, while ordinary reading is less likely to be misclassified.

    Audio is treated as presence. If the active tab is playing audio when the system goes idle, tracking continues. Watching a 40-minute talk is engagement, not idling. A locked screen has no such exemption — if you locked the computer, tracking stops.

    Brief focus loss has a 300 ms grace period. Opening DevTools or a zoom menu can momentarily unfocus the window. Focus Pulse waits 300 ms and re-checks before deciding the browser genuinely lost focus, so these brief interruptions do not unnecessarily fragment tracking.

    Surviving Manifest V3

    Chrome can suspend extension service workers, and in-memory variables can disappear with them. Focus Pulse treats chrome.storage.local as the persistent source of truth and runs a one-minute alarm that checkpoints the session in progress. Without that checkpoint, a long single-tab session — such as a video or long read — could lose time when the service worker is suspended.

    Short visits¶

    A session shorter than 10 seconds is recorded as a short visit and can reduce the focus score.

    A short visit is only counted when the session ends because you deliberately moved away from the page — for example, by switching tabs or loading a new URL.

    A session cut short because the browser lost focus, the system went idle, or the session was checkpointed by the alarm is not treated as a short visit. Those events are interruptions outside the browsing behaviour being measured.

    The focus score¶

    Every day starts at 100 points. The score then loses points for selected browsing behaviours:

    score = 100

       − (tab switches × 1.5)
       − (short visits × 3)
       − (10 if more than 15 distinct sites were visited)

final score = clamped to 0–100 and rounded

    Grades and focus zones¶

    The final score is also shown as a letter grade and a focus zone.

    Score Grade Focus zone
    90–100 A Highly focused
    80–89 B Highly focused
    65–79 C Moderately focused
    50–64 D Moderately focused
    0–49 D Distracted

    The focus zone gives a simple interpretation of the score, while the letter grade provides a more detailed classification.

    Why did my score change?¶

    Focus Pulse makes the score easier to understand by showing a breakdown of where points were lost.

    The dashboard shows:

    • Starting score — every day begins at 100.
    • Tab switching — each tab switch reduces the score by 1.5 points.
    • Short visits — each qualifying short visit reduces the score by 3 points.
    • Website variety — visiting more than 15 distinct sites applies a 10-point penalty.
    • Today's focus score — the resulting score after the penalties are applied and the value is clamped between 0 and 100.

    This breakdown is intended to make the score transparent rather than presenting it as an unexplained number.

    Your Personal Baseline¶

    After enough daily data has been collected, Focus Pulse calculates a personal baseline using up to the previous seven days of focus scores.

    At least three previous days are required before a baseline is shown. This prevents the comparison from being based on too little information.

    The dashboard compares today's score with your usual score and shows whether you are:

    • Above your usual focus
    • Below your usual focus
    • Around your usual focus

    The personal baseline is calculated entirely from your locally stored daily statistics.

    Focus Insight¶

    Focus Pulse also provides a short Focus Insight based on the day's browsing statistics.

    The insight can highlight patterns such as:

    • Frequent tab switching
    • Several short visits
    • A large number of different websites
    • A score significantly above or below your personal baseline
    • A generally strong or steady focus score

    The insight is intended as a lightweight suggestion, not as a measurement of your actual productivity or mental attention.

    For example, a high number of tab switches may result in a suggestion to keep fewer active tabs. A score above your personal baseline may result in a positive message encouraging you to continue the browsing routine that worked well.

    These insights are generated locally and do not require your browsing data to be sent anywhere.

    Limitations of the score¶

    The focus score is a v1 heuristic, not a scientific measurement of attention, productivity, or concentration.

    The score measures observable browsing behaviour such as tab switching, short visits, and website variety. It cannot determine whether you are actually focused on a task.

    For example, reading a long document without switching tabs may produce a high score even though Focus Pulse cannot know whether you were paying attention. Similarly, switching tabs frequently may be completely reasonable when a task requires research.

    The personal baseline and Focus Insight provide additional context, but they do not change this fundamental limitation.

    The formula may evolve in future versions as Focus Pulse develops more meaningful ways to represent browsing behaviour.

    Where the data lives¶

    Each day is stored as a single small record in chrome.storage.local:

    Field Contents
    sites Hostname → milliseconds of focused time
    switches Number of tab switches
    shortVisits Number of sessions under 10 seconds
    maxTabs Highest simultaneous tab count that day

    Alongside these sits the in-progress session state — the current hostname and when its session started.

    The daily records are used to calculate the focus score, 7-day trend, personal baseline, and Focus Insight.

    The maxTabs value is tracked for informational purposes but is not currently used directly in the focus-score formula.

    Because several listeners can fire at once — for example, a tab switch, tab close, and alarm — read-modify-write operations on the daily record are serialised through a queue. This prevents concurrent updates from overwriting each other and losing recorded activity.

    Nothing here is transmitted anywhere. See Privacy for the full statement.
